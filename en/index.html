The method

How to study with these cards — The method

Five steps

  1. Read the question on the front.
  2. Answer before you turn: in your head, aloud or in writing.
  3. Turn the card and compare.
  4. Rate yourself from 1 to 4: 1 didn't remember · 2 partially · 3 correct but unsure · 4 correct and confident.
  5. Next time, review the cards you rated 1 or 2 first.

Key point

Answer before you turn. In controlled studies, recalling an answer led to better long-term retention than reading it again.

Source: Roediger & Karpicke 2006

PER-V1-M5-P49Module 5 · IndicesFront

Question

Cairo Recession Types

How does the Cairo system classify gingival recession, and what does each type predict?

Try to recall

  • Cairo is based on the ___ attachment level
  • RT1, RT2, RT3 (interproximal vs buccal loss): ___
  • What the type predicts: ___

Answer

Cairo Recession Types — Answer

Cairo et al. (2011) proposed classifying gingival recession by the interproximal clinical attachment level. The 2018 World Workshop adopted the same three recession types in its case definitions for mucogingival conditions.

  • RT1: gingival recession with no loss of interproximal attachment; the interproximal CEJ is clinically not detectable at either the mesial or the distal aspect of the tooth
  • RT2: interproximal attachment loss less than or equal to the buccal attachment loss
  • RT3: interproximal attachment loss greater than the buccal attachment loss
  • Interproximal loss is measured from the interproximal CEJ to the base of the interproximal sulcus or pocket; buccal loss from the buccal CEJ to the apical end of the buccal sulcus or pocket.

What the type predicts

  • RT1 (Miller Class I and II): complete root coverage can be predicted
  • RT2 (overlapping Miller Class III): randomised trials indicate a limit of interdental attachment loss within which complete coverage remains predictable
  • RT3 (overlapping Miller Class IV): complete root coverage is not achievable

Key point

Cairo classifies recession by interproximal attachment level relative to the buccal, and the type carries a prognosis for root coverage. Recording the recession type is therefore part of treatment planning, not merely a description of the defect.

Sources: Cairo 2011 · Cortellini & Bissada 2018

PER-V1-M6-P51Module 6 · Clinical casesFront

Question

Clinical Case

Build a complete diagnosis. 30-year-old, systemically healthy, non-smoker. Interdental CAL 1–2 mm; fewer than 30% of teeth affected, none showing greater attachment loss. RBL ~12%, coronal third. Maximum PD 4 mm; no tooth lost to periodontitis. No vertical bone loss ≥ 3 mm, Class II/III furcation involvement, ridge defect or Stage IV rehabilitative-complexity features. Full-mouth BoP 20%, including bleeding at sites probing 4 mm.

Try to recall

  • Form and stage (+ why): ___
  • Grade (+ why): ___
  • Extent + status: ___

Answer

Case: Stage I-B Localized — Answer

Localized periodontitis, Stage I, Grade B; BSP status: currently unstable.

Working it through

  • Confirm: interdental CAL at ≥ 2 non-adjacent teeth, or buccal/oral CAL ≥ 3 mm with pocketing ≥ 3 mm at ≥ 2 teeth, not ascribable to non-periodontal causes → periodontitis
  • Extent: fewer than 30% of teeth → localized
  • Stage: interdental CAL 1–2 mm, RBL coronal third (< 15%), no tooth loss due to periodontitis, maximum PD 4 mm, no Stage III/IV complexity factors → Stage I
  • Grade: no direct evidence of progression available; indirect evidence RBL % ÷ age = 12 / 30 ≈ 0.4 (0.25–1.0) → Grade B
  • BSP status: bleeding at sites probing 4 mm → currently unstable

Key point

Stage I is the earliest detectable periodontitis: the interdental attachment loss is what separates it from gingivitis. Grade B is the starting assumption and is kept unless evidence shifts it. Status is a separate axis from stage — a Stage I case can be unstable.

Sources: Papapanou 2018 · Tonetti 2018 · Dietrich 2019

Question

Clinical Case

Build a complete diagnosis. 42-year-old, systemically healthy, non-smoker. Interdental CAL ≥ 5 mm at 30% or more of teeth (worst site 6 mm). RBL ~55%, middle/apical third. Maximum PD 7 mm; vertical bone loss ≥ 3 mm; Class II furcation at one molar; 2 teeth lost to periodontitis; masticatory function preserved; no Stage IV rehabilitative-complexity features. Full-mouth BoP 45%.

Try to recall

  • Form and stage (+ why): ___
  • Grade (+ why): ___
  • Extent + status: ___

Answer

Case: Stage III-C Generalized — Answer

Generalized periodontitis, Stage III, Grade C; BSP status: currently unstable.

Working it through

  • Confirm: interdental CAL at ≥ 2 non-adjacent teeth, or buccal/oral CAL ≥ 3 mm with pocketing ≥ 3 mm at ≥ 2 teeth, not ascribable to non-periodontal causes → periodontitis
  • Extent: 30% or more of teeth → generalized
  • Stage: interdental CAL ≥ 5 mm, RBL in the middle/apical third, 2 teeth lost to periodontitis (≤ 4), complexity factors present (PD ≥ 6 mm, vertical bone loss ≥ 3 mm, Class II furcation); masticatory function preserved and no Stage IV rehabilitative complexity → Stage III
  • Grade: no direct evidence of progression available; indirect evidence RBL % ÷ age = 55 / 42 ≈ 1.3 (> 1.0) → Grade C. No smoking or diabetes grade modifier applies
  • BSP status: pocket depths ≥ 5 mm present → currently unstable

Key point

Stage III may include the loss of up to 4 teeth due to periodontitis when Stage IV rehabilitative-complexity features are absent. Stage IV can be assigned even with fewer than 5 periodontitis-related lost teeth when the case requires complex rehabilitation because of Stage IV complexity features. Grade C here rests on indirect evidence alone, a bone-loss/age ratio above 1.0; grade modifiers can raise a grade, they are not required to reach one.

Sources: Papapanou 2018 · Tonetti 2018 · Dietrich 2019

Evidence

How the sources are handled

Every clinical statement is checked against the source it cites. If a statement cannot be verified, it is removed: fewer statements, each one certain.

  • A

    Class A · PubMed-indexed scholarly literature: studies, reviews, consensus reports and indexed clarifications.

  • B

    Class B · Formal guidelines and official documents of scientific societies or intergovernmental bodies, even when indexed.

  • C

    Class C · Non-indexed historical or reference sources.

  • A class C source can document history or naming, but it is never the only support for a clinical criterion.
  • The card shows a short source line. Full references, with DOI, are listed at the end of each module.
